Why does billboard advertising work near Addison?

The typical Dallas–Fort Worth commute is about 27.8 minutes each way. A roadside screen is the one ad they can't skip, block, or scroll past.

Dallas–Fort Worth drivers spend close to half an hour commuting, a little above the national figure. That makes the surrounding driving environment relevant when planning a campaign near Addison.

How Blip rental works

  • Pay-per-play, not a lease. Traditional rental is usually a fixed multi-week contract on one face. On Blip you set any daily budget, then either let Blip Optimized pick strong signs for you or choose your own boards, and pay for the plays you buy — pause or stop anytime. No long-term lease and no minimum spend.
  • Digital rotation. Each board runs a loop of short spots (typically about 7.5–10 seconds). Your ad shares the loop with other advertisers instead of owning the face full-time.
  • Availability can change. Counts above are a snapshot, not a hold. You’ll see what’s open when you build a campaign.

Addison Billboard Advertising FAQs

Do I need a contract or minimum spend to advertise in Addison?

No. Blip is self-serve with no minimum spend and no long-term contract. You set a daily budget, then either let Blip Optimized pick signs for that budget or choose your own boards. You can pause or stop your campaign at any time.

Can I pick exactly which Addison boards my ad runs on?

Yes — choose I’ll Choose My Own Signs when you set up, and pick specific boards from the Addison map and board list on this page (street address, size, and estimated daily impressions among adults 18 and over, adjusted for share of voice). Or use Blip Optimized and we’ll select strong signs in your target area for your budget.

Why do two billboards on the same road cost different amounts?

Blip charges per play, and every face has its own audience and its own demand. Two boards that look similar can differ by facing, direction of traffic, and advertiser competition. Confirm live play prices when you build a campaign — see the Texas page for statewide measured dispersion examples.
