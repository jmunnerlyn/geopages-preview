How much does a billboard cost in Texas?

Here’s what recent Texas daily budgets delivered on roadside digital billboards. With Blip there’s no minimum spend: you set a daily budget and pay per blip — a 7.5–10 second ad display on a rotating digital board. Price per blip varies by board, time of day, and advertiser demand. These are observed outcomes from recent Texas roadside campaigns, not fixed packages:

These are medians from recent roadside digital billboard campaigns (place-based screens excluded), not a fixed rate card; higher-spend campaigns often ran on more expensive boards or hours.

A lower price per blip does not always mean cheaper reach — cost per 1,000 views depends on how many people see each play (buyer-scoped CPM), not the play price alone.

There’s no single answer to how much billboards cost in Texas — what a board costs depends on its traffic, the hours you run, and how many other advertisers want the same screen. Traditional billboard rental locks you into a fixed multi-week contract on one board; with Blip you pay per play across as many boards as you like, and you can stop any time. Texas billboard advertising FAQs

›Do I need a contract or minimum spend to advertise in Texas?

No. Blip is self-serve with no minimum spend and no long-term contract. You set a daily budget, then either let Blip Optimized pick signs for that budget or choose your own boards. You can pause or stop your campaign at any time.

›Can I pick exactly which Texas boards my ad runs on?

Yes — choose I’ll Choose My Own Signs when you set up, and pick specific boards from the Texas map on this page (location, size, and estimated daily impressions among adults 18 and over, adjusted for share of voice). Or use Blip Optimized and we’ll select strong signs in your target area for your budget.

›Why do two billboards on the same road cost different amounts?

Blip charges per play, and every face has its own audience and its own demand. Across Texas boards over the last 30 days, neighboring faces within half a mile typically differed by about 1.5× in cost per play — and sometimes more than 4×. The largest factor is how many people actually see that specific face, including which direction it reads. Two sides of a single structure can look identical and deliver very different traffic: one League City structure has two 36×10 faces about 40 feet apart, and the busier-facing side costs roughly 4.7× the other because it sees about 3.2× the audience.
