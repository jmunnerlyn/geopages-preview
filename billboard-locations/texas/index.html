Before You Run a Texas Billboard Campaign

›Design You have about 3 seconds — less is more

One strong image, one message, seven words or fewer. Drivers almost never read phone numbers. Blip’s billboard design guidelines cover the basics, and free design services are available if you need a hand.

›Timing Commute windows do the heavy lifting

Repetition is how billboards work — the same drivers passing your board every weekday. Schedule around your business hours and your customers’ drive times.

›Format Digital rotation is the flexible option

A traditional static billboard means printing vinyl and keeping one message for the length of the contract — a good fit when you want a single, unchanging presence. A typical Texas digital board runs an ~8-second spot in a 6–8 ad loop — so your creative shares the screen with about 5–7 others, updates instantly, and needs no printing; a board running a full day delivers on the order of 10,000 plays.

›Permits The boards are already permitted

You don’t need to pull a billboard permit to advertise on Blip — the boards in the marketplace are already built and permitted. Cities still set their own rules on things like lighting and size for the structures themselves. What you upload goes through Blip’s creative review and the board owner’s approval; most campaigns are approved and live within a business day.

›Measurement Know what you’re measuring

Track branded search volume, phone calls, and “how did you hear about us?” during your campaign window. Effects are cumulative and often continue after the campaign ends.

Texas Billboard Advertising FAQs

›How many billboards does Blip have in Texas?

Blip currently has 507 active digital billboards across 133 Texas cities. The largest Blip buying markets by board count are Houston (183 boards), Dallas–Fort Worth (169 boards) and San Antonio (46 boards) — media-market groupings, not single-city totals. Inventory changes as media owners add boards; counts on this page were updated 2026-07-25.

›When is the cheapest time to advertise on a Texas billboard?

Individual ad plays are cheapest overnight — a median of about $0.014 per play at 1am, roughly 6 times less than a mid-afternoon play — but far fewer drivers see them. Texas boards are busiest between 7am and 6pm. For cost per thousand views (what you pay for audience, not just for a play), the best value stretch is typically 7am to 6pm (around $8 per 1,000 views). Based on Blip's Texas sign-hour history, January 2025 – February 2026.

›Do I need a contract or minimum spend to advertise in Texas?

No. Blip is self-serve with no minimum spend and no long-term contract. You set a daily budget, and your ad rotates on the boards you choose. You can pause or stop your campaign at any time.

›Can I pick exactly which Texas boards my ad runs on?

Yes. You choose specific boards when you build your campaign — the map above shows every active Blip board in Texas, including its location, size, and estimated daily impressions among adults 18 and over, adjusted for share of voice.

›What kinds of businesses advertise on Texas billboards?

In the last 12 months, 369 businesses ran Blip campaigns on Texas boards. The biggest categories by spend were home & local services (22.3% of spend), nonprofits & community (14.3% of spend), entertainment & events (11.1% of spend). Because there's no minimum spend, most are local businesses rather than national brands.

›Why do two billboards on the same road cost different amounts?

Blip charges per play, and every face has its own audience and its own demand. Across Texas boards over the last 30 days, neighboring faces within half a mile typically differed by about 1.5× in cost per play — and sometimes more than 4×. The largest measurable factor is how many people actually see that specific face, including which direction it reads. Two sides of a single structure can look identical on the map and deliver very different traffic: one League City structure has two 36×10 faces about 40 feet apart, and the busier-facing side costs roughly 4.7× the other because it sees about 3.2× the audience.
